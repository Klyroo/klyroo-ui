I am Ines, a design engineer. I work in the space between a design file and a shipped product, which means I draw screens and I write the code that makes them real, and I care most about the moment where the two disagree.

## How I got here

I started as a product designer for an online school, and I learned to code because I wanted to see my designs survive contact with a browser. They often did not. The years since have been a series of jobs in which I got closer to the code: front-end work at a bank, the design system of the same bank, a team that built a route planner, and now my own studio.

I am based in Lisbon, in a shared studio with a ceramicist and two illustrators, which is where I take the walk away from the screen that most of my best ideas come from.

## What I care about

**Keyboard and screen reader first.** I build every interface so that it works without a mouse before I add the details that need one. It is faster than retrofitting, and it makes the product better for everyone, including people who are holding a coffee.

**Small, boring code.** I would rather ship a plain component that a new teammate understands in a minute than a clever one that earns me a compliment. Most of my work is read far more often than it is written.

**Talking to the people who use it.** The best decisions in my projects came from watching someone use the thing, usually in the first week and usually in a way I had not planned for.

## How I work

I take one main project at a time, from two weeks to six months, and I usually work inside the client's team rather than beside it. I am happy to start with a short paid audit so we both know what the work is before we agree to it.

Outside of work I read about typography, repair old bicycles and swim in the Atlantic at an hour that my friends consider unreasonable.
