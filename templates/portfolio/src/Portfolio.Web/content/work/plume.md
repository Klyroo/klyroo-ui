Halden Academy sends about 400,000 emails a week: lesson reminders, receipts, certificates. They were templates in a repository, and changing a sentence meant asking an engineer and waiting for a release.

## The problem

Engineers did not want to be the editors of the copy, and the content team did not want to touch HTML that a mail client could break. Both sides had been burned: a broken receipt had gone out to 30,000 students, rendered fine in the preview and badly in one popular mail app.

## What I did

I was hired to design the editor and, once the screens were done, stayed to build them. That turned out to matter, because most of the design decisions were really constraints of email.

- **Blocks, not a canvas.** Editors assemble an email from a short list of blocks (heading, text, button, lesson card). Each block is an MJML component with variants that were tested in the clients that matter, so a block cannot be configured into a broken state.
- **Previews you can believe.** Every draft is rendered in nine clients before it can be published, and the page shows them side by side with the differences marked. A publish is blocked on a rendering error, not on a warning.
- **Copy in one place.** Text lives in the editor and the data in the template, so a receipt can be reworded without a deploy while the amounts stay generated by the code.

::: warning
Dark mode in mail clients is not one thing. Each client inverts colors differently, so the blocks use colors tested in each of them instead of relying on a media query.
:::

## Result

Copy changes went from a median of three days to under an hour, and no email has gone out broken since launch. The content team now writes new campaigns on their own, and the engineers got their afternoons back.
